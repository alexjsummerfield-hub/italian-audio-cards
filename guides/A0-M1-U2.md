Goal: Greet someone, exchange names, and manage a first encounter.

Prerequisites: A0-M1-U1 sound awareness.

### The pattern

Italian distinguishes tu, informal singular you, from Lei, polite singular you. Use the formal set for a polite first encounter and follow the other person's lead as the relationship develops. Voi addresses more than one person; you will learn its verb forms later.

The capital L in Lei can make polite address clear in writing; lowercase is also used. It does not represent a different spoken word from lei, she. Context and the verb help identify the meaning.

For now, mi chiamo, ti chiami, and si chiama are whole chunks. Do not try to generalise their pronouns yet. Scusa and mi scusi are also chunks; the command system is later material.

### Examples and communication

- Buongiorno. Good morning.
- Mi chiamo Giulia. My name is Giulia.
- Piacere. Nice to meet you.

Informal encounter:

— Ciao! Mi chiamo Marco. E tu?
— Mi chiamo Giulia. Piacere.
— Come stai?
— Sto bene, grazie.

Formal encounter:

— Buongiorno. Come si chiama?
— Mi chiamo Maria.
— Piacere. Come sta?
— Sto bene, grazie.

Greeting customs and the switch from buongiorno to buonasera vary by place and situation. You do not need a fixed clock time or a rule about cheek kisses to greet someone appropriately.

Quick reference: Informal: ciao, tu, come stai. Polite singular: buongiorno/buonasera, Lei, come sta.
