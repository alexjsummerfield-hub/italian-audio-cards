## A1-M2-U3 Possessives

Goal: Identify whose things they are and talk about family relationships.

Prerequisites: Gender, articles, plurals, family, avere and essere.

### Vocabulary preview

Active: possessives in the table below. No new noun set is required. Di now also links a thing to its owner: il libro di Maria, Maria's book.

### The pattern

A possessive agrees with the thing or person possessed, not with the owner's gender. Il suo libro can mean his book or her book. La sua casa can mean his house or her house. Context identifies the owner.

- Owner — Masculine singular — Feminine singular — Masculine plural — Feminine plural
- my — mio — mia — miei — mie
- your, informal singular — tuo — tua — tuoi — tue
- his/her; your, polite singular — suo — sua — suoi — sue
- our — nostro — nostra — nostri — nostre
- your, plural — vostro — vostra — vostri — vostre
- their — loro — loro — loro — loro

Pass 1 uses mio/mia, tuo/tua, suo/sua. Pass 2 adds nostro/nostra, vostro/vostra, loro. Pass 3 adds the plural columns. All forms are introduced here, but they need not be drilled together immediately.

For ordinary things, use article + possessive + noun: il mio libro, la tua casa, i nostri libri, le loro macchine. Loro never changes, but the noun and article do.

### Family pattern

With common unmodified singular family nouns, normally omit the definite article before mio, tuo, suo, nostro, and vostro:

- mio padre, my father;
- tua madre, your mother;
- sua sorella, his/her sister;
- nostro figlio, our son.

Keep the article with plural relatives and with loro: i miei fratelli, my brothers; la loro madre, their mother. Modified and affectionate family expressions have additional patterns; this unit practises the plain forms above. Amico/amica follows the ordinary article pattern: il mio amico, la mia amica. Reference: Treccani on possessives and family nouns.

### Examples and communication

- La mia macchina è piccola. My car is small.
- Sua madre è insegnante. His/her mother is a teacher.
- I nostri figli sono studenti. Our children are students.
- Il libro di Maria è qui. Maria's book is here.

— Tua sorella è in Italia?
— Sì, è a Milano.
— Ha una casa?
— Sì, la sua casa è grande.

Common mistake: A woman's book is still il suo libro, because libro is masculine. Do not infer the owner from suo versus sua.

Quick reference: Agree with the possessed noun. Normally retain the article; omit it in the plain singular family pattern, except with loro.

### Ready for audio practice

Use ownership prompts that identify the owner. Delay plural possessives until the singular pattern is comfortable.

- Type — Prompt — Model
- Core — My house is big. — La mia casa è grande.
- Variation — Their mother is French. — La loro madre è francese.
- Integration — Our daughters have three books. — Le nostre figlie hanno tre libri.
