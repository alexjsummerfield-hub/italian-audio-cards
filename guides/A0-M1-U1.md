## A0-M1-U1 Sounds and spelling

Goal: Hear and reproduce common Italian sounds and use spelling to support listening.

Prerequisites: None. This unit introduces sound recognition, not sentence construction.

### Vocabulary preview

The words in this table are recognition sound examples. You are not yet expected to translate them from memory. Their ordinary meanings are included so that they are not meaningless syllables.

- Word — Meaning — Listen for
- casa — house — clear a vowels, hard c
- sera — evening — clear e
- vino — wine — clear i
- nome — name — clear o
- uno — one — clear u
- cena — dinner — soft c
- chi — who — hard ch
- gatto — cat — hard g and doubled t
- giro — turn or tour — soft g
- funghi — mushrooms — hard gh
- bagno — bathroom or bath — gn
- famiglia — family — gli
- scena — scene — sc before e
- scuola — school — sc before u
- anno — year — doubled n
- caffè — coffee — doubled f and final stress
- città — city — doubled t and final stress
- perché — why or because — final stress

### Vowels and consonants

Italian has five vowel letters: a, e, i, o, u. Standard pronunciation distinguishes open and closed versions of stressed e and o, giving seven stressed vowel sounds. Start by keeping vowels clear and steady. Avoid the large vowel glides common in English, and do not routinely turn unstressed vowels into the English weak “uh” sound. Listening matters more than naming the sound. Reference: Treccani on Italian phonology.

- Spelling — Usual sound — Example
- c before a, o, u — k — casa
- c before e, i — English ch — cena
- ch before e, i — k — chi
- g before a, o, u — hard g — gatto
- g before e, i — English j — giro
- gh before e, i — hard g — funghi
- ci or gi before a, o, u — soft c or g; i often signals the sound — ciao, giorno
- sc before e, i — English sh — scena
- sc before a, o, u — sk — scuola
- sch before e, i — sk — schema
- gn — one joined palatal sound, roughly ny — bagno
- gli in words such as famiglia — a joined palatal l sound — famiglia
- qu — roughly kw — quattro

New recognition examples here: ciao hi/bye, giorno day, schema scheme or diagram, quattro four. The i in ci/gi is not always silent; learn the particular word from audio. The gli spelling also has exceptions, so do not force one pronunciation onto every word containing those letters. English “ny” and “lli” comparisons are starting aids, not exact equivalents.

h has no sound of its own in ordinary Italian words. It helps mark hard c/g and distinguishes some written verb forms. r is tapped or rolled; begin with a light tongue contact rather than an English r. s and z can have different sounds in different words. Follow the model audio rather than assuming one sound for every occurrence.

### Double consonants

- A doubled consonant is longer than a single one. Hold the consonant or its closure a little longer
- do not simply make the whole word louder. Recognition pairs: pala shovel / palla ball
- ano anus / anno year. These pairs show why length can change meaning. They are listening examples, not active vocabulary targets.

### Stress and written accents

Many words stress the next-to-last syllable: ca-sa, pa-ro-la, bam-bi-no. Recognition meanings: parola = word; bambino = child. Others do not: TA-vo-lo means table. Learn stress with the word rather than treating the common pattern as a rule without exceptions.

Final stress is normally written in words such as caffè, città, and perché. In ordinary spelling, internal stress usually is not marked. Accents also distinguish short words: è = is; e = and. Use the written forms given here, including è, sì yes, and perché. Reference: Treccani on written accents.

### Alphabet reference

Use this when learning to spell names; it is not a memorisation requirement before the next unit.

- Letters — Italian names
- a b c d e f g — a, bi, ci, di, e, effe, gi
- h i l m n o p — acca, i, elle, emme, enne, o, pi
- q r s t u v z — cu, erre, esse, ti, u, vu, zeta

The traditional alphabet has 21 letters. j, k, w, x, y also occur in loanwords and names; recognition names include i lunga, cappa, doppia vu, ics, ipsilon.

Quick reference: Listen for clear vowels, hard/soft c and g, consonant length, and stress. Italian spelling is helpful but does not reveal every pronunciation detail.

### Ready for audio practice

Use three short passes: vowels; consonant combinations; stress and doubles. Hear a word, pause, reproduce it, then compare. Later, read a familiar sound example aloud before hearing it. English-to-Italian sentence production is not required here.

- Core: Hear casa → reproduce casa.
- Variation: Hear cena, then chi → preserve the different c sounds.
- Integration: Read caffè → say it with doubled f and final stress.

Move on once the distinctions are familiar. Pronunciation will improve through the rest of the course.
