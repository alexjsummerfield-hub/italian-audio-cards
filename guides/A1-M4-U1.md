## A1-M4-U1 Time and frequency

Goal: Give a time, describe when an activity happens, and say how often it happens.

Prerequisites: Essere, numbers already introduced, present verbs. This unit has four passes; calendar reference material need not be memorised in one sitting.

### Vocabulary preview

Pass 1 active numbers:

- Range — Forms
- 11–15 — undici, dodici, tredici, quattordici, quindici
- 16–19 — sedici, diciassette, diciotto, diciannove
- 20–29 — venti, ventuno, ventidue, ventitré, ventiquattro, venticinque, ventisei, ventisette, ventotto, ventinove
- tens not yet introduced — settanta 70, ottanta 80, novanta 90
- larger numbers — cento 100; mille 1,000; duemila 2,000

Reuse zero–dieci and venti–sessanta from A1-M2-U2. Combine a ten with a unit: trentadue, 32; quarantacinque, 45. Drop the final vowel of the ten before uno and otto: trentuno, trentotto. Compounds ending in tre take an accent: trentatré. Numbers normally do not change for gender. In front of a singular noun, one uses the article-like forms: un libro, una casa.

- Pass 2 active time language: l'ora → le ore, hour/time
- il minuto → i minuti, minute
- un quarto, a quarter
- mezza, half in the time expression
- meno, minus/to
- a che ora, at what time
- alle, at the in the taught clock pattern
- all'una, at one o'clock
- mezzogiorno, noon
- mezzanotte, midnight.

Pass 3 active days and frequency:

- Italian — English
- lunedì, martedì, mercoledì — Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday
- giovedì, venerdì — Thursday, Friday
- sabato, domenica — Saturday, Sunday
- il giorno → i giorni — day
- la settimana → le settimane — week

Pass 4 calendar and routine context: il mese → i mesi, month; la mattina → le mattine, morning; la sera → le sere, evening; la notte → le notti, night; stasera, tonight; poi, then; verso, around, in a time expression; dalle … alle …, from … to … with clock times. Months: gennaio, febbraio, marzo, aprile, maggio, giugno, luglio, agosto, settembre, ottobre, novembre, dicembre. Start with the current month and personally relevant months; expand later.

Recognition: raramente / di rado, rarely; a volte, sometimes; una volta alla settimana, once a week; il pomeriggio, afternoon. These are not required in this unit's production prompts.

### Pass 1 Numbers

Use number combinations with familiar nouns before adding clock patterns. Abbiamo ventidue libri. We have twenty-two books. Continue age practice: Ho ventitré anni. I am twenty-three years old. Do not assume a chart must be memorised before practice starts.

### Pass 2 Clock time

Che ore sono? or Che ora è? asks the time. The answer is usually plural, with sono le:

- Sono le tre. It is three o'clock.
- Sono le tre e dieci. It is ten past three.
- Sono le tre e un quarto. It is quarter past three.
- Sono le tre e mezza. It is half past three.
- Sono le tre meno un quarto. It is quarter to three, 2:45.
- È l'una. It is one o'clock.
- È mezzogiorno. / È mezzanotte. It is noon / midnight.

The feminine article refers to the understood ore, hours. The at-time pattern changes to alle: Parto alle tre. I leave at three. Use all'una, a mezzogiorno, and a mezzanotte for those exceptions. Alle and all'una are taught here as clock patterns; the general combination system comes later.

### Pass 3 Days and frequency

Lunedì lavoro normally refers to Monday in the relevant week. Il lunedì lavoro means I work on Mondays, habitually. Days and months normally start with lowercase letters.

Di solito can go at the beginning: Di solito lavoro a Roma. Usually I work in Rome. Sempre/spesso commonly follows the verb: Leggo spesso, I often read. For never, put non before the verb and mai after it: Non bevo mai il tè. I never drink tea.

Ogni is followed by a singular noun: ogni giorno, every day. Do not add a plural -i just because the activity repeats.

### Pass 4 Calendar and time ranges

Use in + month in the taught pattern: Partiamo in giugno, we leave in June. A simple date is il + number + month: il tre maggio, on May 3. The first of a month uses il primo; primo, first, is introduced here as the date exception.

La mattina and la sera can mean in the morning/evening habitually: La sera leggo. In the evening I read. Di notte means at night. For approximate time use verso: Parto verso le otto, I leave around eight.

Dalle nove alle cinque means from nine to five. Italian also uses the 24-hour clock, particularly in schedules: alle diciassette = at 17:00, five in the afternoon. For clock practice, prompts should identify morning/evening when relevant.

### Examples and communication

- Che ore sono? — Sono le sette e mezza. What time is it? — It is 7:30.
- A che ora parti? — Parto alle otto. What time do you leave? — I leave at eight.
- Lavoro dalle nove alle diciassette. I work from 9:00 to 17:00.
- Di solito studiamo la sera. We usually study in the evening.

Quick reference: It is three = sono le tre. At three = alle tre. Never = non + verb + mai. Every + singular noun.

Unlock number, clock, frequency, and calendar passes separately. Reuse old verbs so the new challenge is time language.
