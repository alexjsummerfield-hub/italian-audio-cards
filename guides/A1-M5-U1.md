## A1-M5-U1 Volere potere and dovere

Goal: Say what someone wants to do, can do, or has to do.

Prerequisites: Infinitives, present verbs, sapere, negation. Reflexives are needed only for the final extension.

### Vocabulary preview

- Active verbs: volere, want
- potere, can/be able to/be allowed to in a situation
- dovere, must/have to. Ripetere, repeat, is introduced here as an infinitive after a modal
- its independently conjugated forms are not required yet.

- Active expressions: Può ripetere? Can you repeat? formal
- Non posso venire, I cannot come
- Devo andare, I have to go. Volentieri, gladly, and purtroppo, unfortunately, are active short responses or sentence adverbs.

### The pattern

- Person — Volere — Potere — Dovere
- io — voglio — posso — devo
- tu — vuoi — puoi — devi
- lui/lei/Lei — vuole — può — deve
- noi — vogliamo — possiamo — dobbiamo
- voi — volete — potete — dovete
- loro — vogliono — possono — devono

Use conjugated modal + infinitive, with no linking preposition:

- Voglio mangiare. I want to eat.
- Possiamo partire. We can leave.
- Devi studiare? Do you have to study?
- Non posso venire. I cannot come.

Only the modal changes for the person. Vogliamo parlare, not vogliamo parliamo.

Sapere expresses learned ability: So leggere, I know how to read. Potere expresses possibility or permission: Posso leggere oggi, I can read today. English “can” alone is ambiguous, so early prompts should state the intended meaning.

Volere + noun is also possible: Vuoi un caffè? Do you want a coffee? Voglio can sound direct in a request to staff. It remains useful for stating a desire. Polite ordering with vorrei comes in Module 7.

### Reflexive extension

Use this only after the basic modal pattern is comfortable. With a reflexive infinitive, replace its final -si with the pronoun matching the person:

Voglio alzarmi, I want to get up; vuoi alzarti, you want to get up; vuole alzarsi, he/she wants to get up; vogliamo alzarci, we want to get up; volete alzarvi, you plural want to get up; vogliono alzarsi, they want to get up.

The pronoun attaches to the infinitive, which loses its final e: alzare + mi → alzarmi. An alternative places the pronoun before the modal: Mi voglio alzare. First practise the attached pattern; recognise both as valid. Do not use both pronoun positions in the same sentence.

### Examples and communication

- Dobbiamo lavorare oggi. We have to work today.
- Maria vuole leggere. Maria wants to read.
- Puoi venire a Roma? Can you come to Rome?

— Vuoi venire a Roma?
— Volentieri, ma devo lavorare oggi. Posso venire domani.

Would you like to come to Rome? — Gladly, but I have to work today. I can come tomorrow.

Quick reference: Modal changes, following verb stays in the infinitive. Learned skill: sapere. Situation or permission: potere.
