## A1-M1-U2 Origin and basic questions

Goal: Say where someone is from and where someone is now.

Prerequisites: Essere, questions, negation, e and ma.

### Vocabulary preview

- Active: Roma Rome
- Milano Milan
- Sydney Sydney
- Italia Italy
- Australia Australia
- Francia France
- Toscana Tuscany
- qui here
- chi who
- dove where
- di dove where … from
- come how
- di of/from in the origin pattern
- a at/in with cities
- in in with countries and regions.

Recognition: che cosa / cosa what. These become productive with everyday verbs in A1-M3-U1.

### The pattern

- Essere di + city: origin. Sono di Milano. I am from Milan.
- Essere a + city: present location. Sono a Milano. I am in Milan.
- Essere in + country or region: location. Sono in Italia. I am in Italy. Sono in Toscana. I am in Tuscany.

These are specific patterns, not a complete preposition system. For a country of origin, use nationality for now: Sono australiano, rather than generalising di to every country name. Countries have other article patterns that will be introduced where needed.

Question words normally come before the verb:

- Question — Meaning — Possible answer
- Chi è? — Who is he/she? or Who is it? — È Maria.
- Dove sei? — Where are you? informal — Sono a Roma.
- Di dove sei? — Where are you from? informal — Sono di Sydney.
- Di dov'è? — Where are you from? formal; also where is he/she from? — Sono di Milano.
- Come stai? — How are you? informal — Sto bene, grazie.

Di dov'è joins dove + è with an apostrophe. Come stai and sto bene remain the already introduced greeting chunks; the verb stare is developed in Module 3.

### Examples and communication

- Giulia è di Roma, ma è a Milano. Giulia is from Rome but is in Milan.
- Siamo in Australia. We are in Australia.
- Non sono qui. They are not here; context identifies they.

— Di dove sei?
— Sono di Sydney. E tu?
— Sono di Milano.
— Sei in Italia?
— Sì, sono a Roma.

Common mistake: Di Roma and a Roma express different things. Origin is not the same as current location.

Quick reference: di + city = origin; a + city = location; in + country/region = location.

### Ready for audio practice

Vary the person and place, then contrast origin with location. Include polite questions with the third-person singular form.

- Type — Prompt — Model
- Core — I am from Rome. — Sono di Roma.
- Variation — Where are you, plural? — Dove siete?
- Integration — Maria is Australian, but she is in Italy. — Maria è australiana, ma è in Italia.
