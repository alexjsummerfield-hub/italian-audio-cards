# Content notes

48 curated cards: 7 MODEL, 7 CORE, 24 VARIATION, 6 INTEGRATION, 4 REVIEW. Editorial approval is for this sample, not an external Italian-language review.

The full companion guide/blueprint was not available as a source file. The preparation guide is therefore the explicit prerequisite contract for this standalone sample. No possessive adjectives, modal verbs, past tenses or unexplained idioms are required. Possession means having, expressed with avere. Review is limited to essere, adjective agreement and the introduced chunk a casa.

Subject pronouns may be included or omitted; lui/lei in model answers clarify the English subject. Figli means children or sons depending on context. No browser speech recognition; simple text comparison is available after keyboard dictation. Accepted answers are illustrative, not an exhaustive grading key.

First practice: all seven forms modeled before 20 production cards, weighted toward CORE. Later practice: 20 cards favoring VARIATION and cumulative material. Session length counts production cards; first-practice models are extra. Composition is a prototype default to refine by listening.

## Prompt wording

Routine instructions such as Listen & Repeat belong in the card heading, never in the spoken prompt. Optional `prompt_note` is displayed after the sentence in brackets and is not spoken. Use it only when the sentence/context does not resolve number, register or speaker gender; bare English you still needs disambiguation. Where a group is named in the sentence, do not add a redundant plural note. All production cards now give a single direct English sentence; former transformation cards are reclassified as VARIATION. Keep formal address out of this sample as stated in preparation.

## Dictated answer flow

Sentence practice is the default. Production prompts wait indefinitely for an answer; there is no timed reveal or automatic advance. The single-line Italian text field supports the iPhone keyboard's own dictation, without a custom microphone or browser speech recognition. The learner finishes dictation then presses Return. Submitting always reveals and plays Italian, including for a matching answer. Introductory MODEL cards remain optional listen-and-repeat preparation.

Comparison ignores case, common dictation punctuation and extra spaces, but preserves Italian accents (e and è are different words). Known optional subject-pronoun alternatives are stored explicitly. A mismatch means different from stored answers, not proof of bad Italian or poor pronunciation. Dictation is an indirect aid, not a pronunciation score. Typed/dictated responses remain in memory on the current card and are not saved or sent by the app; the device keyboard handles dictation.

Return submits the single-line answer field and dismisses the keyboard. There is no check button or dictation helper paragraph. Enter used to confirm an in-progress composition does not submit; held-key repeats are ignored. Native form submission uses the same answer handler.
